MY ROLE / MY REASONING / WHAT I WOULD TEST

I focused on the handoff.

I designed and built this interaction to explore one question: can I make a pension request easier to follow for the member and the team handling it?

01 / THE PROBLEM I EXPLORED

I made the next action explicit.

I put the task, its reason and who acts next together. I kept progress visible without inventing a completion date or service promise.

02 / THE PATTERN I REUSED

I connected the two views.

I use the same status and history in both perspectives. The administrator’s action updates the member’s explanation, rather than creating a separate version of the truth.

03 / THE CHOICE I KEPT

I kept help alongside the task.

I used plain language, labelled controls, visible keyboard focus and a route to human support. I kept the task manageable without hiding important context.

04 / THE RESULT I WOULD TEST

I would measure understanding.

I would test whether people can identify the next action, complete the sample task and explain what happens afterwards. Fewer incomplete submissions and status enquiries are hypotheses, not measured results.

My assumptions and how I would validate them

I based this exploration on the role’s focus on members, administrators, accessibility and consistent patterns, alongside XPS’s public administration materials. I have not inspected its private platforms or established that this journey is missing.

I would first confirm the real quotation journey, evidence requirements and escalation routes with administrators and compliance specialists. I would then test with members with varied digital confidence and access needs, including keyboard and screen-reader users.

The address-check step, statuses and sample review rules are design fixtures. They are not XPS policy, an identity check, a real quotation or financial advice.
