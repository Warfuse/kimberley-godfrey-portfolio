# What I would validate next

## Before refining the flow

I would work with an XPS researcher, administrator, content specialist and relevant compliance colleagues to understand the actual retirement quotation journey. I would confirm the required evidence, terminology, privacy constraints, exceptions, contact routes and platform capabilities. The sample address check must not be treated as an established requirement.

## My first usability sessions

I would recruit a small initial round with varied pension knowledge, digital confidence and access needs, including people using assistive technology. I would include administrators who handle requests. I would treat this as exploratory research, not a representative quantitative outcome study.

Tasks I would observe:

1. Explain what this request needs and who acts next.
2. Choose and review the clear sample before sending it.
3. Explain what happens after submission, without assuming it is already approved.
4. Review the case as an administrator and describe the member-facing consequence.
5. Recover from the unreadable-sample route.
6. Find an alternative to completing the task online.

## Measures I would collect

- Unassisted task completion and points where help is needed.
- Whether people can correctly explain the current status and next owner.
- Errors, repeated submissions and misunderstood labels during the task.
- Keyboard and screen-reader barriers.
- Qualitative confidence and comments, without treating a confidence score as proof of comprehension.

I would compare design iterations with the researcher and document the changes. In a real service, a later controlled pilot could examine incomplete submissions and avoidable status enquiries against an agreed baseline. This demo has no baseline, real users or measured impact.
